Community event leadership playbook

A planning system that connects partner commitments to clear decisions and day-of ownership.

New illustrative work sample · November 20, 2026

Connected to my ALSO leadership, which included board responsibilities and campus collaboration. The event and operating assumptions below are newly created examples.

A clear operating brief

Roots & Routes is a fictional campus community potluck planned over four weeks. The target is 60 guests in a room approved for 75 people. Four cohost representatives, an event lead, a finance lead and four volunteers bring planned occupancy to 70. Registration closes at 65 guests, the room limit after accounting for those ten organizers.

The assumed budget is $600. Venue costs are $100, shared food and drinks $270, supplies $65 and accessibility support $85. Those commitments total $520, leaving an $80 contingency. The finance lead records paid and committed amounts in the same ledger to prevent double counting.

Four weeks from scope to delivery

WhenAccountable ownerDeliverable and gate
Week 1Event leadAgree on purpose, room capacity, accessibility needs and partner roles. Confirm venue approval before advertising.
Week 2Cohost representativesConfirm contributions and program slots in writing. Publish one registration link and one shared event brief.
Week 3Finance leadReconcile commitments against the $600 ceiling. Check registrations against room capacity and planned food quantities.
Week 4Volunteer captainWalk through arrival, seating and cleanup. Assign named owners to the checklist and confirm the contact tree.

Decision log

  • Venue confirmation is late. Hold promotion until approval arrives. The event lead checks an alternative room within the same budget and capacity assumptions.
  • A cohost asks for another performance. Keep the 90-minute program fixed. Offer a replacement slot or combine activities after the existing owners agree.
  • A $45 supply request arrives. The finance lead checks necessity and existing commitments. If approved, contingency falls from $80 to $35; the ceiling stays $600.

A usable day-of plan

Use the 90 minutes deliberately. Arrival and orientation take 15 minutes, shared food and conversation 30, cohost activities 30, reflection 10 and closing instructions 5. Setup and cleanup are separately assigned outside the guest program.

At the readiness check, each owner reports ready, blocked or decision needed, with a next action and deadline. A task is complete when its evidence is recorded, such as a venue confirmation or a checked room layout.

Within two days, reconcile expenses and record attendance against registrations. Capture one change for the next event from guest and partner feedback. This playbook defines the review; it does not claim the fictional event occurred.
