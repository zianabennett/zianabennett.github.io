Fix the Capacity and Ownership Before Promotion

My ALSO leadership involved meetings, board responsibilities and campus collaboration. This planning exercise applies those coordination skills to Roots & Routes, a campus community potluck with a four-week lead time.

The plan targets 60 guests and reserves room for 10 organizers. Registration closes at 65 guests, keeping total occupancy within the assumed 75-person room limit.

The organizers are four cohost representatives, an event lead, a finance lead and four volunteers. The event lead owns the final scope decision; each owner records commitments and raises exceptions before promotion or spending.

Resolve Requests Without Quietly Expanding the Plan

  • Venue confirmation is late. Hold promotion. The event lead checks an alternative room within the same budget and capacity assumptions.
  • A cohost requests another performance. Keep the 90-minute program fixed. Offer a replacement slot or combine activities after the existing owners agree.
  • The $45 supply request arrives. The finance lead checks necessity and existing commitments before approving the use of contingency.

Make Readiness Observable on Event Day

Use the 90 minutes deliberately. Arrival and orientation take 15 minutes, shared food and conversation 30, cohost activities 30, reflection 10 and closing instructions 5. Setup and cleanup are separately assigned outside the guest program.

At the readiness check, each owner reports ready, blocked or decision needed, with a next action and deadline. Completion needs evidence, such as venue confirmation or a checked room layout.

Within two days, reconcile expenses and compare attendance with registrations. Capture one change for the next event from guest and partner feedback. The operating brief must be updated if the venue, budget or organizers change.
