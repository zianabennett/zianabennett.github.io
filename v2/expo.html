A confirmation is a commitment, not a completion check

My BEES finance and event-coordination experience connected financial responsibility with the practical work of bringing businesses into an event. This new exercise asks what I would want an organizing team to see six days before event day.

The readiness rule checks setup information, a unique booth assignment, an arrival plan and approved power when power is required. Of twelve confirmed vendors, six meet every condition. An invitation is excluded from that readiness denominator because participation is still undecided.

The operating decision Resolve the first blocking dependency for each vendor and assign one owner to the next action. A single readiness percentage cannot tell the team what to do.

Make the unresolved work actionable

Alder and Ink lacks setup information, a booth and an arrival confirmation. Requesting the missing requirements comes first; placement depends on those details. Crescent Bakes needs a specific power decision, while two other vendors share booth B06.

The duplicate booth keeps both vendors blocked even though their other fields are complete. Moving one to a compatible available booth would resolve that conflict for both. The current model can detect the duplicate; a logistics lead must still verify physical compatibility.

Count promised places before counting empty booth IDs

The scenario has fourteen places and twelve confirmed vendors. Three invitations remain open. Confirming all three would exceed capacity by one, even though some confirmed vendors still lack assignments.

The event lead should review commitments before additional confirmations. A waiting process or a verified capacity increase could address the final invitation. The model does not select a business to exclude because it contains no agreed selection criteria.

Show financial exposure before authorizing it

The forecast combines mutually exclusive cost states. Paid expenses are $515, committed but unpaid costs are $560, and remaining estimates are $160. Together they total $1,235, leaving $115 below the $1,350 ceiling.

That baseline already exceeds the original category plan by $35. Supplies and refreshments merit targeted review, while favorable variances elsewhere offset part of the increase. A modeled $80 power cost and $50 supplies cost raise the forecast to $1,365, which is $15 over the ceiling.

The approval implication The full additional exposure needs at least $15 in verified offsets or additional funding. Contingency is an allowance within the ceiling, not another expense to add to the forecast.

The next version would add booth characteristics and an audit trail for each change. It would retain human ownership of commitments and spending decisions. The existing rule checks cover duplicate assignments, conditional power approval and financial-state reconciliation.
