Match Outreach to Intake Capacity

The selected outreach plan fits the budget but almost fills the appointment calendar. Release it in stages and check actual bookings before generating more demand.

More Inquiries Create More Follow-Up Work

My 2024 externship involved peer-nonprofit benchmarking, outreach and donor-engagement analysis, and marketing recommendations. This new case extends that strategy question into an operating model for a small adult-learning program.

BridgePath has a $1,500 cash ceiling, sixty outreach and intake hours, thirty appointment slots and twenty-four learner seats. Teaching is already funded. The objective is a first teaching session attended, rather than a contact, click or booked appointment.

The model compares 144 bounded channel combinations; forty-three satisfy its planning limits. The selected mix uses three partner activations, one $300 paid-search test and one print round. Under the stated assumptions, it forecasts 17.74 first-session starts.

The Calendar Is Tighter Than the Budget

The $1,440 allocation includes $760 of outreach, $200 of shared setup and a $480 learner-support reserve held for all twenty-four seats. Existing wages and funded teaching are outside this cash calculation.

Staff time includes triage for every inquiry and forty-five minutes for every booked intake. A missed appointment still consumes its reserved slot. A reschedule needs another slot, even when some staff hours remain available.

The Decision Treat the calendar as an operating constraint. A financially feasible plan should pause when the team cannot provide a timely intake.

A Small Modeled Advantage Is Not a Strong Real-World Winner

The event-led alternative uses two partner activations, one community event and two print rounds. It forecasts 17.46 starts at $1,470 and 57.35 staff hours. Its 0.28-start difference from the selected plan is too small to justify dismissing events without actual response data.

The recommendation is sensitive to partner attendance. Reducing that assumption from 80% to 60% lowers the original mix to 14.29 expected starts. Reallocating toward the event-led option produces 15.16 under the same lower-attendance scenario.

What This Changes Measure referral quality and attendance early. The exercise identifies an assumption worth testing; it does not establish a causal channel advantage or a guaranteed enrollment total.

Release Outreach Only When the Service Can Absorb It

Begin with one partner activation and the capped search test. That smaller release forecasts 12.60 bookings and 7.24 starts, leaving room to observe the actual response.

At the midpoint, review unique inquiries, program fit, booked appointments, attendance and first-session starts by source. Deduplicate repeat contacts and record reschedules separately. Confirm the remaining calendar and funded seats before releasing each additional activity.

The strategy memo gives prospective learners a clear intake route and keeps donor messaging separate. A learner should be able to understand the schedule and first conversation without navigating a donation request. Sample copy and operating targets remain proposals for the fictional program.

The next iteration would replace assumed response rates and handling time with observed values. Linear response, limited channel caps and the absence of audience-overlap effects are material limits of this starting model.
